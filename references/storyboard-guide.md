# Storyboard guide

## Recommended structure

1. Hook: pose a precise contradiction or question in 0–3 seconds.
2. Intuition: show the familiar phenomenon before terminology.
3. Mechanism: animate the causal chain in two or three scenes.
4. Evidence: display one sourced example, experiment, chart, or comparison.
5. Boundary: explain when the rule fails or what is commonly misunderstood.
6. Payoff: answer the opening question in one memorable sentence.
7. Sources: show compact references and attribution.

## Scene design rules

- Express one sentence and one visual action per scene.
- Let motion explain change, direction, sequence, scale, or causality.
- Use text for labels and conclusions, not full paragraphs.
- Give numbers a baseline, unit, date, and comparison.
- Prefer meaningful transitions: zoom into a mechanism, follow a signal, or transform one representation into another.
- Reserve decorative motion for pauses; it must never compete with the explanation.

## Review questions

- Can a viewer understand the main idea without narration?
- Can a listener understand it without seeing every label?
- Does each visual demonstrate the claim rather than merely decorate it?
- Is the analogy clearly distinguished from the underlying mechanism?
- Does the ending answer the exact opening question?
